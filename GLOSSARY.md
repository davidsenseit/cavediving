# Cave diving terminology

## Language-independent terminology policy

This is the repository's canonical vocabulary for lessons, quizzes, scenarios and interactive tutoring. Use the English terms below unchanged when teaching in another language. Explain their meaning in the learner's language; do not replace them with informal substitutes. Ordinary grammatical plurals and sentence capitalization are allowed. Recognize listed aliases in learner answers, but use the canonical form in feedback. Do not assess spelling instead of conceptual understanding.

Define unfamiliar concepts before assessing them, without revealing the answer to a pending question. If a definition would disclose that answer, teach the concept first and then use a different question. A learner may start with any question; the assistant must still check prerequisites.

This vocabulary standard does not establish universal navigation procedures. Local and training conventions must be identified explicitly. All technical definitions remain **draft pending independent expert review**. This is a concept reference, not an installation procedure or operational dive plan. See the [review record](reviews/2026-11-04-terminology.md).

## guideline

- **Definition:** A physical line used as a route reference in an overhead environment.
- **Function and context:** Provides a reference that can be followed when visual navigation is unavailable.
- **Distinctions:** A guideline may be permanent or temporary. A map, light beam or visible but disconnected line does not establish a continuous route to the team's verified exit.
- **Usage variations:** “Cave line” and “guide line” occur in other material; use guideline here.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## main line

- **Definition:** The principal installed guideline within the part of the cave being discussed.
- **Function and context:** Provides the reference route from which other lines or passages may branch.
- **Distinctions:** It need not begin in open water and does not by itself establish the team's usable exit.
- **Usage variations:** Mainline is an accepted alias; use main line in authored content. “Main” is relative to the described line network, not a guarantee of safety or priority.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## primary reel

- **Definition:** The reel used to deploy the team's entrance guideline connecting open water with the installed cave guideline.
- **Function and context:** Establishes the entrance connection when the permanent line does not reach open water.
- **Distinctions:** The reel is equipment; the guideline it deploys is the physical route reference. Its role differs from a reel or spool used for a jump or gap.
- **Usage variations:** Capacity and configuration depend on the task and training; this term does not prescribe either.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## jump

- **Definition:** A transition between separate guidelines, typically from a point along one line to a branch line, connected by a temporary guideline.
- **Function and context:** Maintains line continuity when changing to a separate line.
- **Distinctions:** A jump is not a swim across unconnected space. Unlike a T-junction, the lines are not already joined at the transition. See gap for an end-to-end connection.
- **Usage variations:** The word may describe the transition, location or temporary connection. State which is meant. Some regional accounts use jump and gap more loosely; clarify the actual line arrangement.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## gap

- **Definition:** A separation between guideline ends along a route, bridged with a temporary guideline for continuous navigation.
- **Function and context:** Describes an end-to-end connection rather than a transition to a branch from a point along a line.
- **Distinctions:** Use “break in continuity” for a generic interruption whose geometry is unspecified. Distance or visibility alone does not distinguish a gap from a jump.
- **Usage variations:** Intentional gaps may occur across open-water openings; do not assume every gap has that setting. Confirm local usage and the actual arrangement.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## T-junction

- **Definition:** An existing guideline intersection with three connected branches arranged approximately as a T.
- **Function and context:** Creates a branch-selection decision on both entry and return.
- **Distinctions:** Connected lines do not identify the correct exit branch. A Y-junction has a different layout; a jump connects initially separate lines.
- **Usage variations:** “T”, “T intersection” and “T junction” are aliases. The label describes the line arrangement, not necessarily the shape of the cave passage.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## line marker

- **Definition:** A device attached to a guideline to communicate navigational or identification information under an understood convention.
- **Function and context:** Includes directional arrows, cookies and REMs.
- **Distinctions:** Marker type, ownership, placement and local context affect interpretation. A marker does not replace guideline continuity or verify an exit.
- **Usage variations:** Personal, team and permanent/system markers describe ownership or role, not interchangeable marker shapes.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## directional arrow

- **Definition:** An arrow-shaped line marker that communicates direction along a guideline.
- **Function and context:** May be installed as a system reference or used as a personal/team reference.
- **Distinctions:** Its direction does not prove that the indicated route is the team's verified, accessible exit. A cookie has no intrinsic direction; a REM provides a personal directional reference.
- **Usage variations:** “Line arrow” is an alias. Permanent arrow arrangements and additional meanings vary by site; do not infer a universal nearest-exit or jump-location rule.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## cookie

- **Definition:** A nondirectional line marker identifiable to its owner or team.
- **Function and context:** Marks an agreed reference point through its identity and placement.
- **Distinctions:** It has no intrinsic left/right or exit direction. Placement can carry meaning for the team under its agreed procedure; an unfamiliar cookie does not convey that meaning to another team.
- **Usage variations:** Often round, but nondirectional markers can have other shapes. Do not infer meaning from color or shape alone.
- **Review status:** Draft.

## REM

- **Definition:** A referencing exit marker used to record a personal or team exit-direction reference.
- **Function and context:** Combines identification with a directional reference interpreted under the owner's agreed procedure.
- **Distinctions:** A REM is not a public exit instruction for other teams. Unlike a cookie, it can encode direction for its owner; another team must not adopt that direction as its own exit reference.
- **Usage variations:** Often described as a hybrid marker. Marking and orientation conventions must be understood before use; shape alone does not identify the team's route.
- **Review status:** Draft.
