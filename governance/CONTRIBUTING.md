# Contributing and review

## Editorial goals
Create concise, original, English-language explanations that prioritize correct reasoning, safety boundaries and practical learning. Use independent organization and original language; do not paraphrase a third-party manual passage-by-passage.

## Workflow
1. Propose a small topic or correction.
2. Draft knowledge facts, question(s), and links using stable IDs and the metadata schema.
3. Check technical assertions against independently available information, including edge cases and disagreements. Keep a private/editorial evidence record as needed even if public prose contains no bibliography.
4. Request review from a competent diver or subject-matter expert independent of the author. A reviewer must examine assumptions, ambiguity, scope, terminology and safety consequences.
5. Set `status: verified` only after actual review has been documented, with reviewer consent, date and review notes.
6. Reopen review after material corrections or changes.

A technical reviewer does not automatically provide legal clearance. Seek appropriate legal review of publication where needed.

## Intellectual property
Do not upload third-party training manuals, exams, photographs, illustrations or substantial adaptations without rights. Scientific facts and general principles can inform original work, but changing wording alone may still create an unauthorized adaptation. Do not use proprietary exam questions as templates. Preserve appropriate attribution for quoted or licensed material; otherwise omit quotations rather than stripping attribution.

## Safety gates
No calculation utility may be labeled operationally validated until independently tested against documented cases, with explicit assumptions, units, failure scenarios and review. An AI explanation is not a substitute for real-world planning checks.


## Terminology
Use [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) as the shared vocabulary for knowledge, questions, choices, explanations and examples. Use canonical spellings, ordinary plurals and capitalization; aliases aid recognition rather than changing technical meaning. Keep these English terms in other-language tutoring.

When adding a concept, define it in the glossary or linked prerequisite lesson before assessing it. Review ownership, direction, physical continuity and local convention separately. Add genuine usage differences explicitly. Update relevant learning pathways, audit existing occurrences and run the repository checks. For a new glossary entry include Definition, Function and context, Distinctions, Usage variations and Review status. Document evidence and expert-review status; editorial consistency is not technical approval.
