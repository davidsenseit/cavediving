# Return-junction review

## Teaching notes

- Learning objective: Identify the route-selection decision that remains at a connected T-junction.
- Level: Scenario analysis
- Prerequisites: T-junction; guideline continuity; team exit identification; local marker conventions.

## Question

In a tabletop exercise, all three branches of a T-junction are physically connected. The team returns from a side branch, but its route record does not identify which of the other two branches leads to its verified entrance. What is the missing information?

## Choices

A. The length of temporary guideline needed to connect the existing junction.
B. The identity of the return branch leading to the team’s verified entrance.
C. The shortest geometric distance from the junction to any surface opening.
D. The direction chosen by the most recent team to pass the junction.

## Answer and explanation

**B.** The lines are already joined; the unresolved issue is which branch belongs to the team’s return route. A addresses a physical break that the scenario excludes. C substitutes proximity to an opening for a verified usable exit route. D substitutes another team’s route for this team’s route. A complete route record needs continuity and an unambiguous return decision under the agreed navigation convention.

## Follow-up discussion

How would the problem change if the side-branch line stopped short of the main line?

### Tutor guidance

Expect two separate requirements: a suitable physical connection and identification of the correct return direction. Accept jump terminology when consistent with the described geometry.
