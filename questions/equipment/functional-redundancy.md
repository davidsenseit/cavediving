# Lighting configuration review

## Teaching notes

- Learning objective: Identify independent lighting power and distinguish it from an endurance check.
- Level: Configuration application
- Prerequisites: Primary and backup lights; shared power failure; usable duration.

## Question

A conventional open-circuit cave-diving configuration has three light heads. Two use one battery pack and the third has its own battery. All illuminate. Which change addresses the missing third independent power source while leaving duration to be checked separately?

## Choices

A. Supply one of the two shared heads from a separate, compatible battery system.
B. Increase the capacity of the battery pack that supplies the two shared heads.
C. Put a separate switch on each of the two heads using the shared battery pack.
D. Add a spare charged battery in a pocket for replacing the shared pack underwater.

## Answer and explanation

**A.** Separating the supply removes the stated shared-power dependency between those two heads. The resulting arrangement would have three independent supplies, assuming no other shared failure is introduced. B increases stored energy but keeps the dependency. C separates switching, not power. D proposes recovery work after a failure rather than a third independently powered usable light. Compatibility, access, operation and contingency duration still need assessment; the proposed change alone does not approve the configuration.

## Follow-up discussion

Why does confirming that all three heads turn on fail to answer the duration question?

### Tutor guidance

Look for the distinction between operation now and endurance over the planned exposure and contingencies. Do not infer duration from brightness or battery count.
