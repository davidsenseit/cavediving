# Identify the limiting retained supply

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-011 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A has 18 L at 180 bar; B has 24 L at 225 bar. Use fixed exit-demand bounds of one third of each starting inventory, full donor deliverability and 1 bar upward retention rounding. Calculate both thresholds, entry allowances and donor checks. State what limits A's allowance.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Inventories: **3,240** and **5,400 reference L**. Demands: **1,080** and **1,800 reference L**; combined **2,880 reference L**. A needs **160 bar** rather than ordinary 120 bar, allowing **20 bar = 360 reference L** entry. B keeps **150 bar**, allowing **75 bar = 1,800 reference L** entry. Retained supplies: **2,880** and **3,600 reference L**, surpluses **0** and **720 reference L**. A's ability to supply the combined fixed demand limits its entry allowance.

## Follow-up discussion

Can the calculated allowances establish that the two thresholds are reached at the same time?

## Tutor guidance

No. Consumption history and the real shared route are not determined by these independent inventory thresholds. The first applicable limit governs.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
