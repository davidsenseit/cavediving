# Breathing-rate calculation

## Teaching notes

- Learning objective: Convert a cylinder pressure drop to a surface-normalized volume rate.
- Level: Calculation application
- Prerequisites: Total cylinder water volume; reference litres; absolute ambient pressure; normalized rate.

## Question

An ideal-gas classroom record gives 24 L total cylinder water volume, a 40 bar pressure drop over 16 minutes, and constant ambient pressure of 3 bar absolute. Use a 1 bar reference and comparable temperature. Treat all measured gas loss as breathing consumption. What is the surface-normalized volume rate?

## Choices

A. 2.5 L/min.
B. 20 L/min.
C. 60 L/min.
D. 180 L/min.

## Answer and explanation

**B.** Gas used is 24 × 40 = 960 reference L. Dividing by 16 gives 60 reference L/min during the exposure; dividing by the ambient/reference pressure ratio of 3 gives 20 L/min. A reports the numerical pressure-loss rate (40 ÷ 16) with the wrong unit. C omits pressure normalization. D multiplies by the pressure ratio instead of dividing. This stipulated steady record does not establish emergency demand.

## Follow-up discussion

If the same pressure drop and duration applied to 12 L total water volume at the same pressure, what normalized rate would result, and why?

### Tutor guidance

Expected: 10 L/min. Half the water volume means half the gas quantity for the same pressure change. Require the volume argument as well as the number.
