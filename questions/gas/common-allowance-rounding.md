# Round a common entry allowance

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-009 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A and B each have 20 L. A starts at 238 bar; B at 212 bar. Cap the common entry allowance at one third of the lower start, rounded downward to a 5 bar pressure-drop step. Each stipulated exit demand equals that rounded common quantity. Calculate allowance, individual thresholds, combined demand and both retained supplies.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Lower start / 3 = 212 / 3 = **70.666… bar**. Round the permitted drop down to **70 bar**, giving **1,400 reference L** per diver. A threshold: **168 bar**; B threshold: **142 bar**. Combined demand **2,800 reference L**. Retained supplies: **3,360** and **2,840 reference L**; surpluses **560** and **40 reference L**. The 5 bar step applies to the allowance, not to replacing the actual starts.

## Follow-up discussion

If turn readings also had to use 5 bar steps, which direction would you round those?

## Tutor guidance

Upward, preserving or increasing retained supply. Keep this extra condition distinct from the allowance rounding specified in the problem.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
