# Complete the matching worksheet independently

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-011 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A has 24 L at 225 bar; B has 20 L at 240 bar. The exercise stipulates each exit-demand bound at one third of their own starting inventory, fixed when entry use changes. Either diver can lose their entire supply; all remaining donor gas is assumed accessible. Round required retained thresholds upward in 5 bar steps. Produce a labelled worksheet: inventories, individual and combined demands, ordinary and demand-based thresholds, final thresholds, entry allowances and both donor checks. Explain two assumptions that prevent treating the result as a validated dive plan.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Inventories: **5,400** and **4,800 reference L**. Fixed demands: **1,800** and **1,600 reference L**; combined **3,400 reference L**. A compares ordinary 150 bar with 141.666… bar, keeping **150 bar**. B compares ordinary 160 bar with 170 bar, keeping **170 bar**. Both are already 5 bar multiples. Allowed drops **75** and **70 bar**, quantities **1,800** and **1,400 reference L**. Retained supplies **3,600** and **3,400 reference L**, surpluses **200** and **0 reference L**. Fixed fractional demand does not establish route-dependent exit demand; full donor deliverability and lack of additional delay/residual allowances are further assumptions.

## Follow-up discussion

How would you check the arithmetic without merely repeating the same division?

## Tutor guidance

Multiply each retained threshold by capacity, compare with combined demand, and verify retained plus entry quantities equal starting inventory. Then check assumptions independently.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
