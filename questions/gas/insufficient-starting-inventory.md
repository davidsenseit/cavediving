# Recognize an impossible retention requirement

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-007 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A 12 L cylinder starts at 230 bar. A supplied classroom requirement is 2,900 reference L retained. Calculate starting inventory, required pressure and the shortage. Is there a positive entry allowance?

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Starting inventory: 12 × 230 = **2,760 reference L**. Required pressure: 2,900 / 12 = **241.666… bar**, above the start. Shortage: 2,900 − 2,760 = **140 reference L**. There is no positive entry allowance under this model; rounding cannot remove the shortage.

## Follow-up discussion

What if the retention requirement exactly equalled starting inventory?

## Tutor guidance

Zero entry allowance, with no extra margin. Do not change a supplied requirement just to produce a feasible answer.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
