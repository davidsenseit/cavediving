# Test a changed demand assumption

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-008 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A and B each have 20 L at 240 bar. At their ordinary thirds thresholds, each retains 3,200 reference L. An exercise now stipulates combined exit demand of 3,600 reference L, including a delay. Calculate each donor's shortage at the original threshold and the retained pressure needed for the revised requirement. Give the resulting maximum drop from start.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Each donor is **400 reference L short** against the revised bound. Required retention: 3,600 / 20 = **180 bar**. Maximum drop: 240 − 180 = **60 bar**, or **1,200 reference L**. The original 160 bar threshold no longer covers the stipulated demand. Extra delay changed demand, not starting inventory.

## Follow-up discussion

Does this establish that 3,600 reference L is an adequate real-world exit demand?

## Tutor guidance

No; it is supplied by the exercise. A correct division does not validate the delay, breathing rate or route assumptions.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
