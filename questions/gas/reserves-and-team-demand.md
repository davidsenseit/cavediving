# Compare available gas with the actual demand model practice

## Question

A classroom allocation model gives a donor 20 L total cylinder water volume and a starting pressure of 240 bar. The stipulated exit demand for both divers together is 3,520 reference litres. Assume ideal gas, comparable temperature, a 1 bar reference and that the retained donor gas is accessible and deliverable.

Which pressure threshold retains that stipulated quantity, before any additional margins or real-world review?

## Choices

A. 176 bar, retaining 3,520 reference L.
B. 160 bar, because leaving two thirds always covers both divers.
C. 64 bar, because 1,280 L is available for entry use.
D. 152 bar, obtained by halving the combined demand, converting it to a pressure drop, and subtracting from 240 bar.

## Answer and explanation

**A.** Retained pressure is 3,520 ÷ 20 = 176 bar. Independently, the starting inventory is 20 × 240 = 4,800 L; the entry allowance is 4,800 − 3,520 = 1,280 L, corresponding to 64 bar used and 240 − 64 = 176 bar remaining. B leaves only 3,200 L, a modeled shortage of 320 L. C confuses the pressure drop allowed with pressure that must remain. D incorrectly halves a requirement that already includes both divers and produces neither the required pressure nor the correct entry allowance. The correct arithmetic retains exactly the stated demand, with no additional margin; it does not validate the assumptions or establish an operational plan.
