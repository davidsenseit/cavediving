# Recognize an infeasible matching case

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-011 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A has 24 L at 240 bar; B has 12 L at 180 bar. Each fixed exit demand is stipulated as one third of their own starting inventory; all listed donor gas is assumed deliverable. Calculate inventories, combined demand and the minimum pressure B would need to retain that demand. Can the team have positive entry allowance under these assumptions? Explain why changing units cannot fix the result.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Inventories: **5,760** and **2,160 reference L**. Demands: **1,920** and **720 reference L**; combined **2,640 reference L**. B would need **220 bar**, above its 180 bar start. B is **480 reference L short** even before entry. No positive team entry allowance exists under the supplied model. A conversion changes the representation, not the gas available. Reconsider the configuration, plan and demand assumptions rather than forcing a pressure result.

## Tutor guidance

Identify the missing inputs. Do not infer an operational turn pressure from cylinder sizes and fills alone.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
