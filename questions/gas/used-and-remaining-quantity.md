# Separate use from remainder

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-005 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A 24 L configuration starts at 220 bar and now reads 165 bar. Calculate pressure used, gas used, gas remaining and starting inventory. Check that the quantities balance.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Pressure used: 220 − 165 = **55 bar**. Used: 24 × 55 = **1,320 reference L**. Remaining: 24 × 165 = **3,960 reference L**. Starting: 24 × 220 = **5,280 reference L**. Check: 1,320 + 3,960 = 5,280.

## Follow-up discussion

If you convert 1,320 reference L back to bar, is the result the current gauge reading?

## Tutor guidance

It is a 55 bar drop in this configuration; the current reading requires subtracting it from the starting pressure.
Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value. Identify a conceptual error before offering another arithmetic attempt. Do not reveal a pending follow-up before the learner has answered the main calculation.
