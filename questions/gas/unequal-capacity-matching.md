# Find the shortage hidden by equal pressure

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-010 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A has 24 L and B 20 L, both at 240 bar. Stipulate each exit demand as one third of their own starting inventory and hold those bounds fixed. All donor gas remaining is deliverable. Calculate combined demand, B's shortage at an unadjusted 160 bar turn, and both corrected thresholds and entry quantities. Round required retention up to 1 bar steps.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Inventories: **5,760** and **4,800 reference L**. Fixed demands: **1,920** and **1,600 reference L**. Combined **3,520 reference L**. At 160 bar, B has 3,200 reference L and is **320 reference L short**. A keeps **160 bar**, retains **3,840 reference L**, and allows **1,920 reference L** entry. B needs **176 bar**, retains **3,520 reference L**, and allows **1,280 reference L** entry. Donor surpluses are **320** and **0 reference L**.

## Follow-up discussion

Why must B's newly reduced entry allowance not replace its fixed exit-demand bound in this problem?
