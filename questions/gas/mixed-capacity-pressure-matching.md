# Combine capacity and starting-pressure differences

## Teaching notes

Teach GAS-011 first, including its guided calculation with different inputs. Assess units, method, arithmetic and explanation separately. Use the 1 bar reference, ideal-gas approximation and comparable temperature throughout. This is an authored classroom exercise, not a real dive plan.

## Question

A has 24 L at 210 bar. B has 20 L at 240 bar. Each fixed exit demand is one third of their own starting inventory; all donor gas remaining is assumed deliverable. Calculate inventories, combined demand, the stricter of each ordinary thirds threshold and the demand-based threshold, entry quantities and both donor surpluses. Round retention upward to 1 bar steps.

## Response format

Show your calculation with units, label the requested quantities and briefly explain the result. No answer choices are provided.

## Answer and explanation

Inventories: **5,040** and **4,800 reference L**. Demands: **1,680** and **1,600 reference L**; combined **3,280 reference L**. A compares 140 bar with 136.666… bar and keeps **140 bar**. B compares 160 bar with 164 bar and keeps **164 bar**. Entry quantities: **1,680** and **1,520 reference L**. Retained supplies: **3,360** and **3,280 reference L**, surpluses **80** and **0 reference L**. A's lower gauge reading did not mean the smaller starting inventory.

## Follow-up discussion

Which threshold would change if the donor could not deliver all the listed remaining gas?

## Tutor guidance

Accept equivalent exact fractions and decimal notation. Where rounding is specified, assess the conservative direction as well as the value.
