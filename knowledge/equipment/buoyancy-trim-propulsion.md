# Control position before adding tasks

## Core idea

Buoyancy concerns vertical balance; trim concerns body orientation. A diver can hold depth while directing fin wash into sediment. Propulsion must suit the available space and substrate; no named kick is universally suitable. Practice position control and task loading under appropriate supervision.

## Original example

A diver holds depth during a camera task but repeatedly clouds the floor. A stable depth trace alone misses the propulsion and trim problem.

## Distinguish the observations

- Depth behavior shows whether vertical position is being maintained.
- Body orientation and fin clearance help assess trim in the available space.
- Sediment movement helps assess the effect of propulsion and other movement.

These observations answer different questions. A diver can maintain depth with poor orientation, or disturb sediment while turning during a task. Adding buoyant lift is not automatically a correction for either problem. Choose supervised practice that identifies the cause before adding complexity.

## Check understanding

What could a depth trace miss that a teammate can observe directly? Give two examples.

## Learning objective
Explain how weighting, orientation and movement affect control, clearance and sediment.

## Why the distinction matters
Neutral buoyancy balances upward and downward forces at a particular depth. Trim describes orientation; distributing the same weight differently can change orientation without changing its total amount. Propulsion adds movement and water displacement. Correcting one does not automatically correct the others.

Breathing gas has mass. As gas is consumed, the configuration becomes lighter. Exposure protection can change buoyancy with depth. Assess weighting and usable buoyancy across the intended profile rather than judging the configuration only at entry. Weight placement must also allow equipment access and the team's trained failure response.

## Compare two observations
A diver remains horizontal but slowly sinks: investigate vertical balance. Another holds depth but points fins toward sediment: investigate orientation, clearance and movement. Neither observation alone identifies a precise equipment adjustment.

A broad kick may disturb nearby sediment even with good trim. A stationary turn can also move sediment. Choose movements for the actual space; a named technique is not a guarantee of low impact.

## Reasoning task
A diver adds weight to correct a feet-down posture and then needs more inflation to hold depth. Explain why added mass may have changed vertical balance without resolving weight distribution. Equipment adjustments and propulsion require supervised evaluation.
