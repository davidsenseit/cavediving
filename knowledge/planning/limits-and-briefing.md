# A plan needs limits and responses

## Core idea

A useful plan sets boundaries for the route, gas, depth, time, equipment and expected conditions. It also makes clear how the team will communicate and respond when those boundaries cannot be maintained.

Name each time limit explicitly. A latest turnaround time is the deadline for ending penetration and starting the return; a maximum total dive time includes the exit. Allow for the return and contingencies when setting the turnaround deadline. Do not wait until the maximum total dive time to start leaving the cave. Reaching any applicable limit can require an earlier turn, even if other allowances remain.

Ending the dive does not require a vote or a persuasive explanation. Each participant has authority to stop the planned activity, including before entry. Once a termination signal is recognized, the team begins its trained exit response without bargaining for more penetration. Discuss reasons during the debrief, without blame. A planned destination never outranks this decision.

## Original example

Near the planned destination, one diver signals the end of the dive despite having gas above the agreed turn threshold. Reaching the destination is no longer the objective; completing the exit together is.
