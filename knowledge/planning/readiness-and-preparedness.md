# Readiness checks and emergency preparedness

## Learning objective
Explain the distinctions in this lesson and apply them to a changed situation.

## Explanation

Prepare the plan before equipment checks: objective, route, limits, team roles, communication and contingencies. Check the actual equipment and final gas state, not a remembered setup. Equipment matching explains location and function to teammates; a bubble check looks for leakage; trained access and gas-sharing checks address whether the configuration can be used as intended. Resolve discrepancies before entry.

## Why checks complement each other
A packed regulator can be defective. A functioning regulator can have a trapped hose. Accessible equipment can still be unfamiliar to a teammate. One successful check does not make the others redundant.

## Surface preparedness
Plan how to raise the alarm, identify the location, obtain first aid and reach appropriate medical assistance. Consider communication coverage, access and evacuation obstacles, available emergency equipment and people able to use it. An underwater exit is not the end of an emergency if the team cannot obtain help afterwards.

## Worked comparison
Two fictional sites have identical underwater routes. One has nearby road access; the other requires a difficult carry and has no phone coverage. The underwater objective may be the same, but assistance and evacuation arrangements cannot simply be copied between them.

## Practical boundary
This lesson does not prescribe a valve sequence, medical treatment or site-specific rescue plan. Practice the relevant checks and emergency arrangements within appropriate training.
