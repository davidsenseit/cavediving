# Line hazards and followability

## Learning objective
Explain the distinctions in this lesson and apply them to a changed situation.

## Explanation

A guideline must be continuous, identifiable and physically followable by the diver in the actual configuration. A line trap routes a guideline through space a diver cannot follow. Slack can create loops and entanglement opportunities. A connected network also needs an identified return branch. These are separate hazards.

## Worked comparison
In sketch A the line follows a swimmable route. In sketch B a taut section shortcuts a bend through a narrow crevice. Both are connected; only the first is stipulated to be followable. The placement problem in B remains even when visibility is excellent.

## Practical boundary
This lesson supports hazard recognition. Tie-offs, tension, repairs and retrieval require supervised training and agreed team procedures.
