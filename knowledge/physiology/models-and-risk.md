# A dive computer models decompression, not individual safety

## Core idea

A dive computer uses recorded depth, time, configured gases and model settings to calculate decompression guidance. Its displayed limits are not measurements of the diver's actual tissue state or a personal probability of decompression sickness. Remaining within a displayed limit does not eliminate decompression sickness risk. Correct gas settings and device use matter. A symptom concern deserves appropriate medical assessment even when the log appears compliant.

## Original example

Two divers complete a similar profile; one feels unwell. Matching displays do not rule out a medical problem.

Explain the assumption most likely to make the example unsafe if ignored.

## Learning objective
Explain why depth history affects inert-gas loading and why a compliant display does not establish absence of injury.

## The underlying idea
Increased ambient pressure raises the partial pressure of inert gas being breathed. Gas uptake and elimination take time and differ between tissues. A change to shallower depth reduces ambient pressure but does not instantly return the body to its predive state. Decompression guidance models this history using assumptions.

A decompression obligation is not interchangeable with the cave exit: a diver may need both a route out of the overhead and an ascent consistent with the modeled obligation. A plan must account for both, within the diver's training.

Decompression sickness concerns inert-gas effects associated with decompression. Barotrauma concerns pressure-related tissue injury; arterial gas embolism can follow pulmonary barotrauma. These concepts are not diagnoses a learner can reliably distinguish from a brief symptom description. Unexpected symptoms after diving require prompt appropriate medical assessment; computer compliance cannot exclude a problem.

## Compare explanations
'Both computers agree' describes agreement between models and inputs. 'The diver is uninjured' is a claim about a person. Explain why the first does not establish the second.
