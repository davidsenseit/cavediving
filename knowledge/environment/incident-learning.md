# Separate observations from explanations

## Core idea

An incident account may contain measured facts, recollections, interpretations and missing information. Keep these separate. A successful outcome does not prove an adequate margin; a fatal outcome alone does not establish a particular cause. Avoid assigning blame or numerical accident rates without a reliable report and an appropriate denominator.

## Original example

An anonymous post says a diver was rescued after a gas problem. Without a verified report, use it to frame questions, not to publish a confident causal history.


## Check understanding

Explain the assumption most likely to make the example unsafe if ignored.

## Learning objective
Identify opportunities to interrupt a developing incident without inventing its cause.

## Original classroom case
A team adds a photo objective during the briefing. During the dive, one diver becomes occupied with lighting; another assumes the missing acknowledgement was seen. They regroup and end the dive without injury.

Known observations are the added task, uncertain communication and subsequent regrouping. The case does not establish individual motives or prove that a single action caused the event. Identify how workload and unconfirmed communication could interact, and which earlier checks could reveal the mismatch.

## Debrief for learning
Ask what each participant understood at the time, what cues were available, and why the next action seemed reasonable. A good outcome does not prove that the process had sufficient margin. Avoid inventing accident frequencies from illustrative cases.
