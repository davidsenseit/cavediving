# Distinguishing emergency problems

## Learning objective
Explain the distinctions in this lesson and apply them to a changed situation.

## Explanation

Name what has been lost before assuming a response. A lost line means the diver lacks the guideline reference. A missing teammate means team location or contact is unresolved. A broken guideline interrupts continuity. Entanglement restricts movement. Loss of visibility removes visual information without necessarily removing physical line contact. Several can occur together.

## Equipment events
Primary-light failure reduces lighting capability and ends further penetration under the existing course principle. A regulator or manifold fault can affect supply or access to gas; a buoyancy fault changes position-control capacity. A gas-sharing exit requires both an available donor supply and the ability to use it while preserving navigation and team control.

## Original comparison
A diver in zero visibility still has the identified guideline and trained team contact. Another has lost contact with both. The visibility is identical, but the remaining references differ. A description that calls both merely a silt-out conceals a major difference.

## Priorities and boundaries
Breathing, position, the verified exit reference and team communication interact. Assess remaining resources and applicable limits; an apparent fix does not automatically restore the original plan. Search, line repair, disentanglement, valve manipulation and gas-sharing execution are supervised skills. Do not invent a search duration, gas allowance or procedural sequence from this overview.

## Practical learning goals
With an instructor, identify which response belongs to which event, demonstrate it in the intended configuration and review how reduced visibility or another failure changes the task. The theory objective is to distinguish problems and assumptions, not to certify execution.
